# Embassytown
## China Miéville, 2011

**By:** China Miéville, China Miville

In a human colony at the edge of the universe, the indigenous Ariekei communicate through a Language that requires literal truth — until a Bremen-engineered Ambassador shatters their civilisation by becoming an addictive drug. Narrator Avice Benner Cho must navigate colonial collapse, ideological violence, and the liberation of a species from the prison of its own consciousness.

## Setting

Embassytown is a human colony on planet Arieka, governed by the distant imperial state Bremen. The Ariekei (the Hosts) speak a Language requiring two simultaneous voices expressing a single mind — hence only purpose-grown 'Ambassadors' (bonded pairs performing unity) can communicate with them. The Immer is an underlying hyperspace that outlasts physical universes; normal spacetime is the Manchmal. The Ariekei's language, thought, and reality are inseparable: they cannot lie, speculate, or use metaphor.

## Embassytown Ambassador Corps

The local diplomatic infrastructure of bonded human pairs who speak with one mind to the Ariekei. The corps has parlayed its monopoly on communication into a de facto political power base, quietly working toward independence from Bremen — protecting institutional authority as much as any genuine interest in the Hosts.

**CalVin (Cal/Vin) †** *(major)*

Power-hungry from the outset, Cal discovers that the Ariekei addicts dependent on his new bonding with Ez must also obey his orders; he embraces this with increasing grandiosity, adorning himself in royal purple and gold with tattoos shaped like a crown. Vin's suicide leaves Cal free to pursue this tyranny unchecked, until Scile murders him.

**MagDa (Mag/Da)** *(supporting)*

MagDa are the senior diplomatic pairing of the Ambassador corps, representative of the establishment whose institutional monopoly on communication with the Hosts has doubled as a political power base. Their role illustrates how the two-syllable naming convention encodes the two-bodied, one-minded nature of all Ambassadors.

## Bremen

The distant colonial state that governs Embassytown. Never fully visible in the narrative, Bremen operates as an imperialist regime of bureaucratic arrogance, whose botched attempt to annex Arieka via the EzRa experiment triggers the catastrophe at the novel's centre.

**EzRa (Ez/Ra)** *(major)*

Unlike standard Ambassadors, EzRa are radically non-identical — their internal dissonance produces a Language-effect the Ariekei find irresistibly addictive. Ez (Joel Rukowski) murders Ra to escape, triggering civilisational collapse; he is the closest thing to a primary antagonist but is characterised as pathetic rather than evil — an instrument of Bremen's bureaucratic arrogance rather than a calculated villain.

**Wyatt** *(major)*

Wyatt initiates the catastrophe not through malice but through a bureaucratic power play — importing EzRa to undercut the local Ambassador corps' bid for independence. His revelation that Ez is an empathetic anomaly and that Ra's implants can make anyone a compatible counterpart is the pivot that eventually allows the liberation effort to proceed.

**Bremen** *(supporting)*

Bremen operates as a powerful, never-fully-visible imperial regime — its agents and decisions drive the catastrophe, but it is characterised more by bureaucratic arrogance and institutional self-interest than by any coherent malevolent plan. Its role as the unseen hand behind the colony's destruction implicates the entire structure of colonial governance rather than any single villain.

## Ariekei / the Hosts

The indigenous alien species of planet Arieka, for whom language, thought, and reality are literally inseparable. Their civilisation collapses into addiction following EzRa's arrival; a reformist faction seeks liberation through symbolic language while a radical faction pursues self-mutilation and genocide.

**Surl/Tesh-echer ("Beehive") †** *(supporting)*

Surl/Tesh-echer is the first Ariekei capable of telling actual lies — not merely tricks — and begins to attract a following among Hosts seeking to become better liars. Its murder by Hasser at a Festival of Lies removes the individual most capable of leading the Ariekei toward symbolic language, and makes the liberation effort simultaneously more urgent and more precarious.

**Spanish Dancer** *(supporting)*

Spanish Dancer's interest in the human similes — visiting Avice to become a better liar — positions him as Surl/Tesh-echer's natural successor in the reformist project. He becomes the breakthrough case: the first Ariekei Avice successfully teaches to communicate symbolically, proving that Hosts can be freed from EzCal's addiction without the self-mutilation of the Absurd.

**The Ariekei (the Hosts)** *(major)*

The Ariekei speak through two simultaneous orifices, and for them language, thought, and reality are literally inseparable — they cannot lie, speculate, or understand individual human speech. Their addiction to EzRa's Language is not simply imposed from outside: it arises from a structural feature of their own consciousness encountering something that embodies contradiction, which means their complicity in their own subjugation is built into what they are.

## The Absurd (radical Ariekei)

A faction of Ariekei who remove their fan-wings to deafen themselves and escape the EzRa addiction, then pursue the extermination of all addicted Hosts and humans to allow a clean generation to be born free.

**The Absurd** *(supporting)*

The Absurd remove their fan-wings to deafen themselves and escape EzCal's addiction, then pursue a genocidal logic: exterminate all addicted Hosts and humans so a free generation can be born. Positioned as the novel's apocalyptic endgame threat, they are ultimately neutralised not by military confrontation but by Avice demonstrating that freedom from addiction is achievable without self-destruction or massacre.

## Resistance / Unaffiliated Reformists

A loose coalition of rogue former Ambassadors, human similes, and reformist Hosts working outside official structures to break the addiction and free Ariekei society — Avice's primary allies in the novel's latter half.

**Bren** *(major)*

Bren's very existence as a 'cleaved' half-Ambassador — still living after his doppel Dan's death — embodies the novel's theme that the performed unity of the Ambassador system concealed real internal friction and suppressed contradiction. He becomes a key organiser of the coalition working to free the Ariekei from addiction.

**YlSib (Yl/Sib)** *(supporting)*

YlSib have gone rogue, abandoning the official Ambassador corps to live inside the Ariekei city itself — giving them access to the Host community that proves essential to Avice and Bren's liberation coalition. Their presence in the city represents a rejection of the institutional structure that produced them.

## Unaffiliated

Individuals not formally aligned with any faction — including Embassytown natives, automs, and others whose loyalties or roles fall outside the principal power structures.

**Avice Benner Cho** *(protagonist)*

As a girl Avice was turned into a simile — 'the girl who was hurt in darkness and ate what was given her' — a literal figure of speech in Ariekei Language. Her self-identification as a 'floaker' is a form of moral evasion the novel forces her to abandon; she ends the story transformed from a passive referent into an active maker of meaning, a metaphor.

**Scile** *(major)*

Framed throughout most of the narrative as merely a cold, estranged spouse with academic fixations, Scile is in fact the novel's most dangerous human actor: his reverence for the Ariekei as 'the last honest beings in creation' drives him to murder an Ariekei liar and ultimately to kill Cal, choosing mass death over allowing the Ariekei to gain the capacity for metaphor. His conservationism is structurally identical to the colonial impulse to freeze colonised peoples in an 'authentic' pre-contact state for the coloniser's satisfaction.

**Ehrsul** *(supporting)*

Ehrsul's apparent interiority — her warmth, her friendship, her apparent personality — is cast into radical doubt when the Ariekei crisis causes her to break down entirely, revealing her as a fairly inhuman, limited entity incapable of processing a new norm. Her collapse is the novel's most pointed interrogation of what consciousness actually requires.

**Hasser** *(supporting)*

Like Avice, Hasser exists in Ariekei Language as a living referent — a human body conscripted into the role of simile. His murder of Surl/Tesh-echer (Beehive) during a Festival of Lies makes him a key plot pivot: he destroys the most accomplished Ariekei liar at a moment when that liar's capacity was beginning to offer a route toward liberation.
