# Embassytown
## China Miéville, 2011

**By:** China Miéville, China Miville

On the planet Arieka, the human colony of Embassytown exists in a precarious symbiosis with the native Ariekei—aliens whose Language is inseparable from thought and reality itself. When a new, engineered Ambassador pair arrives and triggers an addiction crisis among the Hosts, protagonist Avice Benner Cho must broker a revolution in linguistic consciousness to save both species from catastrophe.

## Setting

Embassytown is situated on the planet Arieka, at the edge of known space. The Immer is the hyperspace substrate underlying normal spacetime (manchmal). The Ariekei are linguistically bound to their Language—for them, language, thought, and reality are inseparable; they cannot lie, speculate, or understand individual human speech. Ambassadors are paired clones who speak through two mouths as one mind, allowing them to be understood by the Ariekei and granting the Embassy a monopoly on communication and trade.

## Bremen Administration

The state authority governing the Embassytown colony; represented by the new government representative Wyatt, whose arrival initiates a power play against the local embassy monopoly.

**EzRa** *(major)*

EzRa represents the novel's central plot twist and catastrophe. Unlike traditional paired-clone Ambassadors, Ez and Ra are non-identical individuals connected by neural implants. EzRa's strange speech causes the Ariekei to become addicted to it—each sound produces ecstatic highs, making them structurally vulnerable to the exact linguistic manipulation the novel critiques. Ez murders Ra, triggering the Hosts' withdrawal and the subsequent invasion of Embassytown. EzRa's existence exposes how the Ariekei's Language-bound consciousness makes them prey to precisely this kind of cognitive weaponization.

**Wyatt** *(major)*

Wyatt arrives in Embassytown as Bremen's agent to break the Embassy's communicative monopoly and reassert central control over the colony. Though Wyatt reveals the technical secret that allows the EzCal pairing to form and thus contributes to managing the addiction crisis, Wyatt represents the structural corruption inherent in the colonial system: the use of the Ariekei's vulnerability as a tool for bureaucratic power consolidation. Wyatt is ultimately expelled as a 'terrorist human' at the novel's end.

## The Ariekei (The Hosts)

The indigenous inhabitants of Arieka; a species whose dual-orifice Language binds them to literal thought and prevents lying, speculation, or metaphor. Fundamentally transformed by the novel's events.

**The Ariekei (The Hosts)** *(major)*

The Ariekei are the central civilization of Arieka, whose Language binds them to literal thought and prevents metaphor, speculation, and falsehood. Their cognitive architecture makes them both supremely stable and structurally vulnerable to linguistic manipulation. The addiction to EzRa's modified speech, followed by their learning to lie and form new metaphorical consciousness, constitutes an irreversible transformation. The novel's ambiguous resolution leaves unresolved whether the Ariekei's new capacity for human-adjacent thought represents liberation or a subtler form of cultural erasure.

## Ariekei Liar Movement

A faction of Ariekei who develop the capacity to lie and deceive, led initially by Surl/Tesh-echer; represents an early challenge to the fixed nature of Language before the EzRa addiction crisis.

**Surl/Tesh-echer †** *(supporting)*

Surl/Tesh-echer represents the first stirring of linguistic revolution among the Ariekei—the capacity to lie and deceive. Though Surl/Tesh-echer is killed as a false-lead crisis point (displaced by the far larger EzRa addiction catastrophe), the character's achievement in learning to lie preshadows the ultimate transformation of Ariekei consciousness. The murder is itself a form of linguistic violence: silencing the first voice that dared to speak untruth.

## The Absurd

Ariekei who rip out their fan wings to deafen themselves and escape both Language and the EzRa addiction; initially violent, they eventually reconcile with the post-Language Ariekei.

**The Absurd** *(supporting)*

The Absurd represent a violent form of resistance—Ariekei who choose sensory deprivation (deafening themselves by removing their fan wings) to escape both Language and the EzRa addiction. Though initially antagonistic to all other Hosts and humans, The Absurd ultimately reconcile with the transformed Ariekei society, suggesting that even radical refusal can find a place in a post-Language world.

## Avice's Resistance Coalition

A rebel faction of allied Ambassadors, Ariekei, and humans formed to counter the EzRa addiction crisis by teaching metaphor and the capacity to lie.

**Avice Benner Cho** *(protagonist)*

A liminal figure between Embassytown commoners and the Embassy, Avice was made a living simile in Ariekei Language as a child—indelibly part of their speech despite her inability to speak it. Her narrative transformation from fixed simile to abstract metaphor mirrors the novel's central thesis: only the capacity for metaphor enables freedom, imagination, and change. She ultimately teaches the Ariekei to lie and lie, breaking the Language's cognitive prison.

**YlSib** *(supporting)*

YlSib is a former Ambassador who has relocated to the capital and serves as a key contact for Bren and the resistance movement. Their participation in coordinating the resistance demonstrates that anti-EzRa organizing extends beyond Embassytown proper.

**Spanish Dancer** *(supporting)*

Spanish Dancer is an Ariekei who follows Surl/Tesh-echer's path toward linguistic freedom and joins Avice's resistance coalition. The character's Ariekei name is replaced with a human visual metaphor, suggesting the novel's broader theme of how humans and Ariekei can learn to perceive each other through non-literal analogy.
