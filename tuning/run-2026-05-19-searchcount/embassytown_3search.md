# Embassytown
## China Miéville, 2011

**By:** China Miéville, China Miville

On the distant colony world of Arieka, humans coexist uneasily with the Ariekei — aliens whose Language is so literal it cannot lie, and whose civilization is upended when a new kind of Ambassador becomes an addictive drug. Avice Benner Cho, a native who was once turned into a living simile, must navigate colonial politics, linguistic catastrophe, and her own complicity to help language — and freedom — evolve.

## Setting

The Ariekei speak a Language in which sound and meaning are inseparable from the speaker's intent — they cannot lie, cannot use symbols, and can only understand speech produced by a single mind inhabiting two bodies simultaneously (an Ambassador). Human Ambassadors are therefore genetically and psychologically engineered twins who speak in perfect unison. The Immer is hyperspace — a substrate that underlies and persists through all universes — navigated by trained Immersers like Avice. Embassytown is a human colony at the edge of the known universe, a satellite of the distant colonial power Bremen.

## Embassytown Colony

The human settler community living under Bremen's colonial authority, including locally-bred Ambassadors and long-term residents. Benefits from Ariekei biotech and trade while normalizing the Ambassador system's structural violence.

**Avice Benner Cho** *(protagonist)*

A self-described 'floaker' whose early passivity is not neutrality but the comfortable disengagement of someone who benefits from a colonial system without examining it. Her arc moves from fixed literal simile — 'the girl who was hurt in darkness and ate what was given her' — to active metaphor, as she teaches the Ariekei to lie and thereby catalyzes the birth of symbolic language itself.

**Bren** *(major)*

A cleaved Ambassador who may have realized only after his doppel Dan's death how much he hated him — the friction was always there, normalized by a system that forced two minds to perform as one. He works with Avice and YlSib in the final resistance against EzCal, his post-cleaving autonomy itself a form of liberation the Ambassador system never intended.

**Ehrsul** *(supporting)*

An automaton and Avice's closest friend in Embassytown, Ehrsul occupies a liminal position in the novel's language-and-consciousness themes — a constructed being whose inner life and capacity for Language-reception remain ambiguous.

**Hasser** *(supporting)*

Like Avice, Hasser was instrumentalized by the Ariekei as a living figure of speech — a human body turned into grammar. His murder of Surl/Tesh-echer at the Festival of Lies is an act of violence that forecloses the Ariekei's most advanced experiment in lying, with consequences that ripple through the novel's central crisis.

## Ambassador Corps

Pairs of psychologically and genetically conditioned humans who speak Language in unison to communicate with the Ariekei. Forced to perform seamless unity while privately capable of profound mutual hostility.

**Cal/Vin (CalVin) †** *(major)*

A high-standing Ambassador pair whose bond fractures under the novel's crisis: Vin commits suicide in despair at the civilization's apparent doom, while the surviving Cal joins Ez to form EzCal — an act of pragmatic alliance that ultimately enables both the colony's survival and its further subjugation to addictive Language.

**MagDa** *(supporting)*

A functioning Ambassador pair in Embassytown, MagDa exemplifies the institutionalized norm of two persons conditioned to present as a single seamless individual — a form of systemic violence against personhood that Embassytown's human community has wholly naturalized.

## Bremen

The distant colonial state that governs Embassytown. Extracts value through biotech and trade, and engineers EzRa out of bureaucratic indifference rather than malice — making it the novel's structural, systemic antagonist.

**Scile †** *(major)*

Initially readable as a principled academic conservationist who finds the Ariekei's lie-free Language beautiful and sacred, Scile's love is revealed as possessive and objectifying — he wants the Ariekei to remain as they are for his own spiritual satisfaction, leading him to murder Surl/Tesh-echer's killer's target and ultimately to kill Cal to ensure the catastrophic conflict continues.

**Ez/Ra (EzRa)** *(major)*

Ez (formerly Joel Rukowski) is an empathetic anomaly whose non-twin pairing with Ra produces speech that acts as a neurological intoxicant for the Ariekei — not through malice but through Bremen's bureaucratic indifference to consequences. Ez is described as 'the closest thing to the work's main antagonist' yet is ultimately pathetic rather than villainous; his final role is producing drug-talk as methadone until the addicted generation dies out.

**Wyatt** *(supporting)*

A former Bremen agent stationed in Embassytown who reveals the technical secret that makes EzRa's addiction possible — and, crucially, that it can be replicated: with Ra's embedded equipment, anyone can become Ez's counterpart. His disclosure enables the formation of EzCal, positioning him as an instrument of Bremen's structural indifference rather than a villain in his own right.

**Bremen** *(major)*

The distant colonial state that extracts value from Arieka through biotech and trade, and engineers EzRa without understanding or caring about the consequences for Ariekei civilization. Bremen's evil is structural and bureaucratic — indifference rather than cruelty — which the novel treats as the more insidious form of colonial harm.

## Ariekei / Hosts

The native alien inhabitants of Arieka, whose literal Language instrumentalizes humans as living similes. Their civilization — biological architecture included — collapses under EzRa's addictive speech.

**Surl/Tesh-echer (Beehive) †** *(major)*

The furthest-advanced Ariekei experimenter in lying — capable of actual falsehood rather than mere tricks — Surl/Tesh-echer's visits to human similes like Avice represent the first crack in the Ariekei's literal Language and the precursor to the full linguistic revolution. Their murder by Hasser at the Festival of Lies is both a tragedy and a structural turning point.

## Ariekei Reformists

A faction of Ariekei led by figures like Spanish Dancer who, under Avice's guidance, learn to use language symbolically — to lie, to metaphorize — breaking free of EzCal's addictive hold.

**Spanish Dancer** *(major)*

A follower of Surl/Tesh-echer's lying experiments who becomes the primary Ariekei recipient of Avice's teaching — learning to use language as a symbolic, referential system rather than a literal one. Spanish Dancer's liberation from Language's cognitive cage is the novel's central redemptive act, transforming the Ariekei's entire communicative and cognitive world.

## The Absurd

A radical Ariekei faction willing to exterminate all addicted Hosts and all humans so a new, immune generation can be born free. Initially mistaken for a rational crisis response; their genocidal plan is revealed late in the novel.

**The Absurd** *(major)*

Initially presenting as a rational crisis-response — the 'drastic but necessary' option — the Absurd are revealed to have a genocidal plan: destroy all addicted Ariekei and all humans so an immune generation can be born clean. Their apparent pragmatism is a misdirection; their true agenda is species-wide extermination masquerading as species survival.
