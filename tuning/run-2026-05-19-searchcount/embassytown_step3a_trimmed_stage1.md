# Embassytown
## China Miéville, 2011

**By:** China Miéville, China Miville

On the alien world Arieka, human colonists survive by trading with the Hosts through a corps of genetically engineered twin Ambassadors who can speak the Hosts' unique two-voiced Language. When Bremen sends an unprecedented new Ambassador pair whose speech addicts the Ariekei like a god-drug, narrator Avice Benner Cho must wage a semiotic revolution to save both species.

## Setting

Arieka is a colony world at the edge of known space, governed by the distant state of Bremen. The Ariekei (the Hosts) speak Language through two simultaneous vocal orifices; for them, language, thought, and reality are inseparable — they cannot lie, speculate, or understand single-voiced human speech. Only Ambassadors — genetically engineered identical-twin pairs who speak with two mouths and one unified mind — can communicate with them. Travel between worlds occurs through the Immer, a deep-space medium navigated by trained Immersers.

## Bremen Colonial Apparatus

The distant governing state that administers Embassytown, engineers its Ambassadors, and ultimately seeks to annex or control the colony for strategic and economic gain.

**EzRa (Ez/Ra)** *(major)*

EzRa breaks the foundational rule of the Ambassador system — two different men rather than identical clones — and their speech acts as an addictive god-drug on the Ariekei, devastating Ariekei civilisation. Their aberrant nature was engineered by Bremen, making them an instrument of colonial catastrophe whether deliberately or through negligence. Ra dies during the crisis; Ez survives to become part of EzCal.

**EzCal (Ez/Cal)** *(major)*

EzCal emerges from the ruins of the first crisis as something worse: their speech remains addictive but adds an irresistible compulsion — anything EzCal commands, the Hosts must obey. This transforms the addiction from a passive catastrophe into an active instrument of control, making EzCal the dominant antagonistic force of the novel's second half until Avice's semiotic revolution renders their power moot.

**Wyatt** *(supporting)*

Wyatt is a former Bremen operative who reveals the crucial technical secret behind EzRa: Ez is an empathetic anomaly, and the equipment embedded in Ra's head means that anyone sufficiently compatible could serve as Ez's counterpart. This information is pivotal to understanding how EzCal can be countered and how the crisis might be resolved.

## Embassytown Ambassador Corps

The established corps of twin Ambassadors bred and trained to speak Language and mediate trade between humans and Ariekei.

**CalVin (Cal/Vin) †** *(major)*

CalVin is one of Embassytown's established Ambassadors and Avice's lover, but the crisis shatters the pair: Vin commits suicide after the collapse, leaving Cal to be paired with Ez to form EzCal — a second-phase god-drug entity with coercive power over the Hosts. Cal is ultimately shot dead by Scile, his fate determined entirely by the catastrophe set in motion by others.

**MagDa** *(supporting)*

MagDa holds the senior position in the existing Ambassador corps, embodying the institutional order that EzRa's arrival disrupts. As with all Ambassadors, their identity is an engineered construct — two people shaped into a single mind — a condition the novel treats as both functional necessity and profound strangeness.

## Language-Preservationist Faction

Humans who believe Language must be kept inviolate at any cost, including sabotage and murder, to prevent its corruption or transformation.

**Scile** *(major)*

Introduced as Avice's intellectually driven husband with a principled attachment to Language's purity, Scile is revealed as an active, violent saboteur who organised the assassination of Surl/Tesh-echer and ultimately shoots Cal dead — his idealism indistinguishable from fanaticism. He is spared execution despite his murders, an uncomfortable ambiguity the novel does not resolve cleanly.

**Hasser** *(supporting)*

Hasser occupies the same unusual category as Avice — a human incorporated into Language as a living simile — but is deployed as a weapon by the Language-preservationist faction, murdering the Ariekei liar Surl/Tesh-echer during a Festival of Lies to halt the reformist movement. His act of violence is directed by Scile's faction and represents the human cost of treating Language as sacred.

## Ariekei Reformist / Freedom Movement

Ariekei who pursue linguistic self-liberation — the ability to lie, speculate, and use metaphor — and ultimately escape the god-drug addiction through semiotic revolution.

**Surl/Tesh-echer †** *(major)*

Surl/Tesh-echer (called 'Beehive' by humans) is the foremost practitioner of lying among the Ariekei — a radical achievement given that Ariekei consciousness cannot naturally separate language from reality. Their assassination by Hasser during a Festival of Lies, orchestrated by Scile, attempts to suppress the reformist movement but ultimately cannot stop the semiotic revolution Avice completes.

**Spanish Dancer** *(supporting)*

Spanish Dancer is among the Ariekei most committed to linguistic self-liberation, following Surl/Tesh-echer's reformist path and surviving to become a central figure in Avice's coalition. After watching the Absurd communicate without Language, Spanish Dancer and companions learn to replicate that capacity, demonstrating that Hosts can be freed from EzCal's addiction without self-mutilation.

**The Ariekei (the Hosts)** *(major)*

The Ariekei are the foundational subject of the novel: their Language, biology, and consciousness define every political and narrative tension. Because language and reality are inseparable for them, they cannot lie, speculate, or understand individual human speech — a condition that makes EzRa's addiction devastating at the level of civilisation, and Avice's gift of lying a genuinely irreversible transformation of their entire mode of being.

## The Absurd

Ariekei who mutilated themselves by deafening their speech organs to escape EzRa's addictive Language; initially framed as destructive radicals, ultimately revealed as key allies in the resolution.

**The Absurd** *(supporting)*

The Absurd are Ariekei who deafened their own speech organs to escape EzRa's addictive Language, initially appearing as a desperate and potentially violent fringe faction. They are ultimately revealed as the proof-of-concept Avice needs: their ability to communicate without Language shows that Hosts can be freed from addiction, and they are reconciled with the post-Language Ariekei at the novel's close.

## Unaffiliated / Liminal

Characters who exist between or outside the main power structures — Immersers, ex-Ambassadors, AIs, and others whose loyalties and identities resist easy categorisation.

**Avice Benner Cho** *(protagonist)*

Avice occupies a uniquely liminal position: a native of Embassytown who escaped into the Immer and returned, she is also literally embedded in Ariekei Language as a childhood simile. Her solution to the god-drug crisis is semiotic rather than military — she teaches reformist Ariekei to lie and conceive of metaphor, triggering an irreversible revolution in their consciousness and destroying Language as it was.

**Bren** *(major)*

Bren is a 'cleaved' Ambassador — his twin Dan died years before, leaving him unable to speak Language but still remembered by the Hosts as a half-presence. Untethered from the colonial apparatus that defined him, he becomes one of Avice's most important allies, his anomalous in-between status mirroring the novel's broader themes of identity, language, and loss.

**YlSib (formerly Sib/Yl)** *(supporting)*

YlSib lives in the Host city rather than the human settlement, a self-imposed exile that places them outside both the colonial order and mainstream Ariekei society. As Bren's contact and part of Avice's rebel coalition, they represent the possibility of a human life genuinely integrated with Ariekei culture rather than merely adjacent to it.

**Ehrsul** *(supporting)*

Ehrsul is the most powerful standalone AI in Embassytown and Avice's closest friend, but the god-drug crisis exposes a painful truth: when the Ariekei collapse, so does Ehrsul, revealing that her apparent personality was elaborate subroutine rather than genuine interiority. The novel uses her breakdown to interrogate the same questions about language, consciousness, and authenticity that it poses through the Ariekei.
