# Embassytown
## China Miéville, 2011

**By:** China Miéville, China Miville

On the remote planet Arieka, a tiny human colony called Embassytown survives by trading with the indigenous Ariekei — beings whose Language is so alien that only specially engineered twin Ambassadors can speak it. When a new, Bremen-engineered Ambassador arrives and his speech acts as an addictive drug on the Ariekei, the colony is plunged into catastrophe. Narrator Avice Benner Cho, a living simile in the Ariekei's Language, must find a way to break the addiction before both species are destroyed.

## Setting

The Ariekei (called 'Hosts') speak a Language in which words are inseparable from their referents — they cannot lie, speculate, or use symbols. Only paired human Ambassadors, whose two voices are perceived as a single minded utterance, can speak Language. Avice and other humans are used as 'similes': living enactments of metaphors the Ariekei need to speak. The colony is a dependency of the distant state Bremen, which controls Ambassador appointments. The 'Immer' is hyperspace; the 'manchmal' is normal spacetime. Arieka sits at the edge of known space, making relief ships rare and political intervention slow.

## Embassytown Colonists &amp; Staff

The human settlers, Ambassadors, and Bremen-appointed administrators who run the colony. Their survival depends on maintaining the fragile communicative relationship with the Hosts, yet their political dependence on Bremen and their exploitation of Ariekei biotech make them complicit in the colony's structural violence.

**Avice Benner Cho** *(protagonist)*

A self-described 'floaker' who prides herself on detachment, Avice is nonetheless the novel's moral engine — her childhood instrumentalisation as a simile gives her a unique, embodied stake in the Ariekei's Language that no Ambassador or linguist possesses. Her arc from passive observer to active revolutionary is complicated by her complicity in the colonial system she ultimately dismantles.

**Scile** *(major)*

An offworlder linguist who married Avice partly for access to Arieka, Scile's reverence for Language as a sacred, unchanging system leads him to secretly support the suppression of Ariekei lying — and ultimately to murder Cal in a futile attempt to stop the linguistic revolution. His idealism is indistinguishable from fanaticism.

**CalVin †** *(major)*

One of Embassytown's most respected Ambassadors, CalVin's intimacy with Avice gives him a personal dimension beyond his institutional role. When Ez's partner Ra dies, Cal pairs with Ez to form EzCal, perpetuating the addiction crisis — a choice that makes him both saviour of the colony's short-term survival and an instrument of the Ariekei's suffering. He is killed by Scile at the novel's climax.

**Bren** *(major)*

Bren's doppel Dan died years before the novel's events, leaving him 'cleaved' — unable to speak Language but still remembered by the Hosts. He is the first to articulate to Avice that Ambassador pairs can harbour mutual hatred beneath their enforced unity, and he becomes a key figure in the resistance against the god-drug.

**Ehrsul** *(supporting)*

Ehrsul's sophisticated turingware makes her appear fully human to Avice, who chooses to treat her as a genuine friend despite persistent doubts about machine consciousness. When the addiction crisis erupts, Ehrsul suffers a kind of cognitive collapse — withdrawing and refusing to process the new reality — which the novel uses to interrogate whether her apparent personhood was always a programmed facade or something more.

**YlSib** *(supporting)*

YlSib is one of the Embassytown Ambassadors who joins Avice and Bren in the effort to free the Ariekei from the god-drug addiction, representing the Ambassador corps' internal fracture between those who perpetuate the crisis and those who resist it.

## Bremen

The distant human state that colonised Arieka and controls Ambassador appointments. Bremen's political machinations — sending EzRa — trigger the catastrophe, and it ultimately grants the colony limited autonomy only because annexation would be too costly.

**EzRa** *(major)*

Unlike all previous Ambassadors — who are cloned or raised as near-identical pairs — EzRa consists of two non-identical individuals whose combined voice produces an unprecedented effect on the Ariekei: ecstatic, compulsive addiction. EzRa is less a character than a political weapon deployed by Bremen, and the two halves are not shown to have chosen their role knowingly.

## The Ariekei (Hosts)

The indigenous sentient species of Arieka, whose Language, biology, and architecture are inseparable from one another. The addiction crisis fractures them into addicted masses, the self-deafened Absurd, and a small vanguard learning to lie.

**Surl Tesh-echer †** *(major)*

Known to humans as 'Beehive', Surl Tesh-echer is the Ariekei who comes closest to mastering symbolic, counterfactual speech — a revolutionary act in a species for whom lying is literally inconceivable. Their growing following among the Hosts represents the first stirring of the linguistic liberation Avice will later complete; their assassination by Hasser is the colony's attempt to suppress that revolution.

**Spanish Dancer** *(supporting)*

Named by humans for the distinctive markings on their body, Spanish Dancer is among the Ariekei most drawn to the similes and to the possibility of lying. They survive the crisis and join Avice's coalition to free the addicted Hosts, representing the Ariekei capacity for self-directed change.

**Addicted Ariekei (collective)** *(supporting)*

The bulk of the Ariekei population, whose biological and architectural world decays in tandem with their addiction — the living city itself becomes infected. They are the primary victims of Bremen's political experiment and the colonial system's structural indifference.

## Human Similes

Humans who were used as children or adults to enact physical metaphors so the Ariekei could speak new concepts. They occupy an ambiguous position: honoured by the Hosts yet instrumentalised by both species.

**Hasser** *(supporting)*

Hasser is one of the humans used as a living simile in Ariekei Language, giving him a peculiar intimacy with the Hosts. His act of murdering Surl Tesh-echer during a Festival of Lies is an act of colonial violence dressed as cultural preservation — eliminating the Ariekei's most dangerous free thinker.

## The Absurd

A faction of Ariekei who deafened themselves to escape the god-drug addiction, sacrificing their ability to speak Language. They represent a radical, painful path to freedom and ultimately reconcile with the post-Language Ariekei.

**The Absurd (collective)** *(supporting)*

A radical Ariekei faction who chose to destroy their own hearing — and thus their ability to speak or receive Language — as the only certain escape from the god-drug. Their self-mutilation is both a survival strategy and a form of mourning for a way of life they can no longer access; they ultimately reconcile with the post-Language Ariekei at the novel's end.
